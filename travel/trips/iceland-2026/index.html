Day by Day

You land at 8 AM Wednesday and fly out Sunday morning. The midnight sun means every hour is usable — golden light at midnight, no rush, total freedom.

1
Wednesday
Land 8 AM — Reykjavik & Grandi Harbor
▾
✈ Arrival · 🏙 Reykjavik

8:00 AM — Land at Keflavik (KEF). Keflavik is a small international airport, but passport control and customs can still take 30–45 minutes depending on the queue. Have your return flight confirmation handy — immigration occasionally asks for it.

~8:45 AM — Pick up rental car. Follow signs to the car rental hall — it's connected to the terminal by a short covered walkway. Budget 30–40 minutes for paperwork, insurance decisions, and vehicle walkthrough. Tip: pre-book online and decline the CDW supplement if your credit card covers it. Do accept gravel/sand protection (SAAP) — Iceland's roads throw rocks.

~9:30 AM — Drive to Reykjavik. 45-minute drive along the Reykjanes coast. The landscape is lunar — black lava fields stretching to the horizon. Stop briefly at the bridge between continents (Sandvík) if you want to stretch your legs.

~10:15 AM — Drop bags at Grandi. Check-in is 3 PM, but the hotel will hold your luggage. You've been traveling all night — freshen up in the lobby bathroom, grab a coffee, and start exploring on foot. The Grandi harbor district is walkable and full of great stuff.

Late morning — Explore the harbor:

— Whales of Iceland (10 min walk): Life-size whale models suspended from the ceiling. Stunning and immersive. Budget 45 minutes.

— FlyOver Iceland (next door): A flying ride simulation over Iceland's landscapes — glaciers, waterfalls, volcanoes. You'll preview everything you're about to see in real life. 20 minutes.

— Lunch at Gló (15 min drive or taxi): Reykjavik's best vegetarian/vegan restaurant. Massive buffet-style spread — roasted root vegetables, curries, grain bowls, raw salads, homemade soups. Everything is plant-based and delicious. ~$18–22/person.

3:00 PM — Check into Grandi. Shower, recharge for 30 minutes.

Afternoon — Reykjavik on foot:

— Hallgrimskirkja Church: Reykjavik's iconic landmark. Take the elevator to the top for a 360° panorama of the city and surrounding mountains. The design is inspired by basalt lava columns — you'll see the real thing on Friday.

— Laugavegur Street: The main walking street. Wool sweaters (lopapeysa), Icelandic design shops, cozy cafés. Stop at Reykjavik Roasters for the best coffee in the city.

— Sun Voyager sculpture: 10-minute walk along the waterfront. A steel Viking ship sculpture facing the midnight sun. Perfect photo with mountains behind it.

Evening:

— Dinner at Kaffi Garðurinn: A beloved vegetarian restaurant on Klapparstígur street. Cozy, candle-lit, with a daily changing menu — mushroom stews, stuffed peppers, hearty grain dishes, homemade cakes. Feels like eating at a friend's house in Reykjavik.

— Alternative: Gló (dinner service) or Veganæs for fully vegan burgers and comfort food.

— After dinner: Walk the waterfront at 11 PM in full daylight. The midnight sun is surreal — golden light, empty streets, mountains reflecting on the bay. This alone is worth the trip.

You'll be tired from travel — don't overdo Day 1. The harbor + Hallgrimskirkja + a good dinner is plenty. Save the big drives for Days 2 and 3.
2
Thursday
The Golden Circle
▾
🌋 Geology · 💧 Waterfalls · 🌿 Geysers

Iceland's most famous route — three world-class sites in a 300 km loop from Reykjavik. With a rental car, you set the pace. Leave by 9 AM, return by 7–8 PM.

Stop 1 — Þingvellir National Park (45 min from Reykjavik)

UNESCO World Heritage Site. Walk between the North American and Eurasian tectonic plates — you're literally standing where continents pull apart. Also the site of the world's oldest parliament (930 AD). The rift valley has dramatic cliffs, fissures, and crystal-clear Lake Þingvallavatn.

— Walk the Almannagjá path (30–45 min, easy, paved).

— For the adventurous: Silfra snorkeling (book in advance with dive.is) — snorkel between tectonic plates in water so clear visibility exceeds 100 meters. 2°C but you wear a dry suit. Once-in-a-lifetime.

— Budget: 1.5–2 hours.

Stop 2 — Geysir Geothermal Area (50 min from Þingvellir)

The original geyser — the word itself comes from this place. Strokkur erupts every 5–8 minutes, shooting boiling water 20–30 meters into the air. Stand upwind. The ground bubbles and steams everywhere — alien landscape.

— Free, parking included.

— Lunch: Geysir Glima restaurant on-site. Excellent vegetable soup, fresh-baked rye bread (húsbrauð baked underground by geothermal heat), Icelandic skyr with berries. Ask for their vegetarian options — they always have a hearty plant-based soup and bread combo.

— Budget: 45 min–1 hour.

Stop 3 — Gullfoss (10 min from Geysir)

Iceland's most powerful waterfall. A massive two-tiered cascade dropping 32 meters into a canyon. The spray catches rainbows on sunny days. Walk down to the lower platform to feel the force — you will get wet.

— Free. Budget: 45 min.

Optional stops (on the way back):

— Kerið Crater: A 3,000-year-old volcanic crater lake. Vivid red and green walls, aquamarine water. Small fee (~$5). 20-minute walk around the rim.

— Secret Lagoon (Flúðir): A natural hot spring pool — much less touristy than Blue Lagoon, more authentic. $25/person. Open until 10 PM. Perfect soak after a day of driving.

Evening: Back in Reykjavik by 7–8 PM. Dinner at Gló or Mat Bar (excellent veggie-friendly tapas and cocktails on Hverfisgata).

Golden Circle total drive: ~300 km / 3 hours driving. With stops, plan 7–9 hours. Roads are smooth, well-marked, easy. Gas up in Selfoss (cheapest on the route). Pack snacks and water — vegetarian options between stops are limited.
3
Friday
South Coast — Waterfalls & Black Sand Beach
▾
💧 Waterfalls · 🏖 Black Sand · 🧊 Glacier Views

The South Coast is Iceland's most dramatic drive. Waterfalls you walk behind, jet-black beach with basalt columns, and glacier views. Leave early — this is the day that makes people come back to Iceland.

Stop 1 — Seljalandsfoss (1.5 hours from Reykjavik)

A 60-meter waterfall you walk completely behind. The path goes through a cave behind the curtain of water — bring your waterproof jacket, you will get soaked. The view from behind, looking out through the falls, is one of Iceland's most iconic images.

— Free to visit. Parking ~$7.

— Hidden gem: Walk 5 minutes south to Gljúfrabúi — a secret waterfall hidden inside a canyon. Wade through a narrow gap in the rocks to find it. Most tourists miss it entirely.

— Budget: 45 min–1 hour.

Stop 2 — Skógafoss (30 min further east)

A perfectly symmetrical 60-meter waterfall — pure power. Walk right up to the base and feel the thundering mist. Climb the 527 steps to the top for a view down the canyon and out to the ocean. Rainbows in the spray on sunny days.

— Free. Parking at the base.

— Lunch tip: Pack a picnic from Reykjavik (Gló or Bónus grocery store) — vegetarian options along the South Coast are very limited. Eat at the Skógafoss parking area with the waterfall as your backdrop. Best lunch view in the country.

— Budget: 1 hour.

Stop 3 — Reynisfjara Black Sand Beach (40 min further east, near Vík)

The most dramatic beach you'll ever see. Jet-black volcanic sand, towering hexagonal basalt columns (Hálsanefshellir cave), and massive Atlantic waves crashing in. The Reynisdrangar sea stacks rise offshore like petrified trolls — Icelandic legend says that's exactly what they are.

— ⚠ Safety: Never turn your back on the ocean here. Sneaker waves are extremely dangerous and come without warning. Stay well back from the waterline. People have been swept away. Enjoy from a respectful distance.

— Budget: 45 min–1 hour.

Stop 4 — Vík (5 min from Reynisfjara)

Iceland's southernmost village. Stop at Súpa in Vík — a small soup restaurant with excellent vegetable and mushroom soups, fresh bread, and a cozy atmosphere. Best vegetarian option on the entire South Coast.

Optional (with midnight sun you have time):

— Dyrhólaey Arch: Massive natural stone arch on a cliff with puffin colonies (June is peak puffin season!). 15 min from Vík. Lighthouse at the top with 360° views — glacier, ocean, black beach, green hills.

Evening: Back in Reykjavik by 8–9 PM. Last dinner at Kaffi Garðurinn or Veganæs (fully vegan comfort food — burgers, loaded fries, shakes). End the night with a midnight sun walk along the harbor — golden light at 11 PM, your final Reykjavik evening.

South Coast total drive: ~360 km / 4.5 hours driving. With stops, plan 10–12 hours. Fill gas in Selfoss or Hella — limited stations after that until Vík. Pack your picnic — don't rely on finding vegetarian food between stops.
4
Saturday
Blue Lagoon & Last Day in Reykjavik
▾
♨ Geothermal · 🏙 Culture

Your last full day. No driving, no agenda, no rush. Soak, explore, eat well.

Morning — Sky Lagoon or Blue Lagoon

— Sky Lagoon (15 min from Grandi): Newer, less crowded, infinity-edge pool overlooking the ocean. Includes a 7-step spa ritual. More intimate, locals prefer it. ~$60–90/person. Right in Reykjavik — easy morning visit.

— Blue Lagoon (50 min drive toward KEF): The iconic milky-blue geothermal spa in a lava field. Bigger, more famous, more crowded. ~$75–115/person. Book in advance — they sell out. Note: since you fly out Sunday morning, doing Blue Lagoon Saturday is better than cramming it on departure day.

Afternoon — Back in Reykjavik:

— Perlan Museum (15 min from Grandi): Built inside hot water tanks on a hill. Indoor ice cave you can walk through, planetarium, exhibits on glaciers and geology. 1.5-hour visit. The observation deck has panoramic views of the city and mountains.

— Harpa Concert Hall (5 min walk from Grandi): Walk through the geometric glass facade — it shifts color with the light. Free to enter. Beautiful architecture.

— Old Harbor: Browse bookshops, design stores, maybe catch a whale watching tour departure. The harbor is at its best on a weekend afternoon.

Evening — Last supper:

— Gló for a final plant-based feast.

— Or: Sumac Grill + Drinks — Middle Eastern / Mediterranean with excellent vegetarian mezze, falafel, and hummus plates. Great for a relaxed last dinner.

— Pack tonight. Set alarms. Your morning flight means an early start.

Book Blue Lagoon or Sky Lagoon well in advance — both sell out, especially on weekends. Sky Lagoon is the better choice for today since it's IN Reykjavik and you don't want a long drive the day before an early flight.

✈ Departure

Early morning — Check out of Grandi. 45-minute drive to KEF. Drop rental car at the airport return lot (follow signs — it's straightforward). Allow 2 hours before departure for international check-in and security.

Breakfast: Grab a quick bite at the hotel before leaving, or eat at KEF — the airport has decent food options including vegetarian choices at Joe & The Juice and the main food court.

Reykjavik to KEF: 45 min. Rental car return adds ~15 min. If your flight is at 9 AM, leave the hotel by 6:15 AM at the latest.

Packing

What to Wear

Iceland in early June: 7–13°C (45–55°F) with wind. It can rain, shine, and blow sideways within the same hour. The key is layering — not bulk. Dress for the waterfalls, the wind, and the walking.

Base Layer
Merino wool thermal top and leggings. Breathable, warm, odor-resistant. Wear under everything.
Mid Layer
Fleece or lightweight down jacket. This is your warmth layer. Zip-up so you can regulate temperature.
Outer Shell
Waterproof, windproof jacket with hood. Gore-Tex or equivalent. The most important piece. Non-negotiable for waterfalls and coast.
Pants
Water-resistant hiking pants or softshell pants. No jeans — they get wet, heavy, and cold. Zip-off pants work well.
Footwear
Waterproof hiking boots with ankle support. You'll walk behind waterfalls, over lava fields, and up 527 steps. Break them in before the trip.
Accessories
Warm beanie, buff/neck gaiter, thin waterproof gloves, sunglasses. The wind at Reynisfjara and Gullfoss is no joke.
Swimsuit
For Blue Lagoon, Sky Lagoon, or Secret Lagoon. Bring a towel too — or rent one at the venue.
Evening Wear
Reykjavik is smart-casual. Clean jeans, a nice sweater, clean sneakers or boots. No formal dress code anywhere.

Reykjavik's top plant-based restaurant. Buffet-style: roasted roots, curries, grain bowls, raw salads, homemade soups. Multiple locations. ~$18–22/person. Eat here more than once.

Practical

Good to Know

Rental Car
Pick up at KEF airport. Standard compact handles all Route 1 roads — no 4x4 needed. Add gravel protection insurance ($10/day, worth it).
Driving
Drive on the right. Speed limit: 90 km/h highways, 50 in towns. Speed cameras everywhere — fines $200+. Gas stations take cards.
Weather
7–13°C (45–55°F). Changes hourly. Check vedur.is each morning. Wind is the real factor, not temperature.
Daylight
Nearly 24 hours of light. Sun barely sets. Bring a sleep mask — blackout curtains help but aren't perfect.
Currency
Icelandic króna (ISK). Cards accepted everywhere — even parking meters. Almost no need for cash.
Budget
Iceland is expensive. ~$50–80/day food, $30–50 activities, $15–25 gas. Blue Lagoon: $75–115. Total: ~$100–155/person/day beyond hotel.
Water
Tap water is glacier-fed and among the purest in the world. Bring a reusable bottle — don't buy bottled, it's the same thing.
Pre-Book
Blue Lagoon, Sky Lagoon, and Silfra snorkeling all require advance booking. Everything else is walk-up.
Veggie Tip
Pack picnic supplies from Bónus or Gló for the South Coast day — vegetarian options between stops are almost nonexistent. Súpa in Vík is the exception.
